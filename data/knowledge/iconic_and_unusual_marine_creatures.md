# Iconic and Unusual Marine Creatures

Jellyfish are not true fish but free-swimming members of the phylum Cnidaria, composed of roughly 95% water and lacking a brain, heart, or blood -- they sense their environment through a simple nerve net and move by rhythmically contracting a bell-shaped body. Most jellyfish sting using specialized cells called nematocysts on their tentacles, delivering venom to stun prey or deter predators; sting severity varies enormously by species, from the barely-noticeable moon jellyfish to the extremely dangerous box jellyfish found in parts of the Indo-Pacific.

Seahorses (genus Hippocampus) are unusual fish that swim upright, lack scales in favor of bony external plates, and are the only animal group where the male carries developing embryos in a specialized brood pouch through pregnancy and gives birth. Seahorses are weak swimmers and often anchor themselves to seagrass or coral using their prehensile tail, ambushing small crustaceans by rapidly sucking them in through a tube-shaped snout.

Manta rays are among the largest fish in the ocean, with wingspans reaching up to 7 meters in the giant manta ray, yet they are filter feeders that pose no threat to humans, straining zooplankton from the water using specialized gill plates as they swim. Unlike stingrays, manta rays lack a venomous barb; they are recognized by distinctive natural spot patterns on their undersides that researchers use to identify and track individuals over years, similar to a fingerprint.

Sea otters are a keystone species in kelp forest ecosystems, using tools (typically a rock) to crack open shellfish while floating on their backs, and consuming roughly a quarter of their body weight in food daily to fuel a very high metabolism needed to stay warm in cold water, since unlike whales and seals they lack an insulating blubber layer and rely instead on extremely dense fur. Sea otter population recovery has directly benefited kelp forest health by controlling sea urchin populations that would otherwise overgraze kelp.

Anglerfish are deep-sea ambush predators best known for a bioluminescent lure (called an esca) that dangles in front of their mouth, generated by symbiotic light-producing bacteria, used to attract prey in the near-total darkness of the deep sea. In several anglerfish species, males are dramatically smaller than females and permanently fuse to a female's body after mating, becoming a parasitic sperm source for the rest of their life -- an extreme reproductive adaptation to the difficulty of finding a mate in the sparse deep ocean.

Sea turtles navigate using Earth's magnetic field, a sense called magnetoreception, which allows females to return with remarkable precision to nest on the same beach where they themselves hatched decades earlier, sometimes traveling thousands of kilometers across open ocean to do so. All sea turtle species are air-breathing reptiles that must surface to breathe despite being fully aquatic, and hatchlings face extremely high mortality in their first year, with only an estimated 1 in 1,000 surviving to adulthood.
