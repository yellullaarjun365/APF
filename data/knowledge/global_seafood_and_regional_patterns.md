# Global Seafood -- Popular Species and Regional Consumption Patterns

General-knowledge reference material on globally significant seafood species and regional consumption patterns, paraphrased from standard fisheries/food-industry overview sources (e.g. FAO global fisheries statistics summaries).

## Globally significant seafood species

Some of the most widely consumed and commercially important seafood species worldwide include:
- **Shrimp/prawns** -- among the most traded seafood products globally, farmed extensively in Asia and Latin America.
- **Salmon** -- both wild-caught (notably Pacific salmon) and farmed (notably Atlantic salmon, farmed heavily in Norway, Chile, and Scotland), a major traded commodity.
- **Tuna** -- several species (skipjack, yellowfin, bluefin, albacore) supply both the canned-tuna market and higher-value sashimi/sushi markets.
- **Tilapia** -- one of the most widely farmed freshwater fish globally, valued for fast growth, hardy tolerance, and mild flavor -- as covered elsewhere in this knowledge base.
- **Cod and other whitefish** (pollock, haddock) -- historically central to North Atlantic and European fisheries and fish-and-chips-style cuisine.
- **Molluscs and cephalopods** -- oysters, clams, mussels, squid, and octopus are significant in many coastal cuisines worldwide.

## Regional seafood and food-culture patterns

Seafood consumption and preference vary widely by region, generally shaped by local geography, available species, and long-standing culinary tradition rather than any single factor:
- **Coastal and island nations** (e.g. Japan, Indonesia, the Philippines, Norway, Iceland) tend to have particularly high per-capita seafood consumption, reflecting easy access to fresh catch and centuries-old culinary traditions built around it.
- **South and Southeast Asia** are major centers of both wild-catch fisheries and freshwater aquaculture (including tilapia, carp, and shrimp farming), given the region's extensive river systems, monsoon-fed water availability, and large coastal populations.
- **Mediterranean cuisines** (Spain, Italy, Greece, Turkey) traditionally emphasize a wide variety of smaller fish, shellfish, and cephalopods, tied to the biodiversity of the Mediterranean Sea.
- **Nordic and North Atlantic cuisines** historically centered on cod, herring, and salmon, reflecting the cold-water species abundant in that region.
- **Landlocked and inland regions** worldwide rely more heavily on freshwater aquaculture (like tilapia and carp pond farming) precisely because ocean seafood requires longer supply chains to reach them -- this is part of why freshwater aquaculture species such as tilapia have become globally important food-security crops, independent of any single country's culture.

## A note on scope

Seafood preference is shaped by geography, history, and available species -- not by any inherent characteristic of the people in a region. Framing it this way (as a matter of local ecology and food supply chains) is the more accurate and useful lens than framing it around "what type of people" live somewhere.
