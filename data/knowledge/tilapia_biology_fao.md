# Nile Tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus) -- Biology & Habitat
Source: FAO Fisheries and Aquaculture Department, Cultured Aquatic Species Information Programme -- Oreochromis niloticus (Rakocy, J.E.), fao.org/fishery/culturedspecies/Oreochromis_niloticus

## Habitat and thermal tolerance
Nile tilapia is a tropical species that lives in shallow water. Its lower and upper lethal
temperature limits are approximately 11-12C and 42C. Its preferred temperature range for
growth is 31-36C. Below the lower lethal limit or above the upper limit, the fish cannot
survive; growth slows well before either extreme is reached.

## Feeding
Nile tilapia is an omnivorous grazer. In the wild it feeds on phytoplankton, periphyton,
aquatic plants, small invertebrates, benthic fauna, and detritus with its associated
bacterial films. It can also filter-feed by trapping suspended particles (phytoplankton,
bacteria) in mucus in the buccal cavity, though most of its nutrition comes from grazing
periphyton mats. This omnivorous, low-trophic-level diet is part of why tilapia farming
has comparatively low feed-cost requirements versus carnivorous farmed fish.

## Reproduction
Sexual maturity in pond culture is typically reached at 5-6 months of age. Spawning
begins once water temperature reaches approximately 24C. The male establishes a
territory, digs a crater-like spawning nest, and guards it -- uncontrolled reproduction
in grow-out ponds is a known management issue, which is why many commercial operations
use monosex (all-male) stocking.

## History and farming context
Nile tilapia culture dates back to ancient Egypt (bas-relief evidence in tombs over
4000 years old showing fish kept in ornamental ponds). It is now one of the most
widely farmed freshwater fish species globally, valued for rapid growth, tolerance of
a range of water conditions, and ease of culture at both subsistence and commercial
intensity levels.
