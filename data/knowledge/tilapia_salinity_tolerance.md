# Nile Tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus) -- Salinity Tolerance

Paraphrased from published research on salinity tolerance and preference in Nile tilapia, including Lawson & Anetekhai (2011, Asian Journal of Agricultural Sciences).

## Nile tilapia is a freshwater species with some salinity tolerance

Nile tilapia is native to and adapted for freshwater, but it is more salt-tolerant than many other freshwater fish species. Laboratory tolerance trials found that hatchery-reared Nile tilapia fingerlings survived fully at salinities from 0 up to about 7 parts per thousand (‰), while complete mortality occurred at 9-10‰ (for comparison, full-strength seawater is roughly 35‰, so this tolerance is still well within the "low brackish" range, not true seawater tolerance). Behavioral responses to threat and feeding varied across this salinity range, and although fish tolerated up to 7‰, the most *preferred* salinity in that study was much lower, around 1‰ -- meaning fish can survive moderate salinity but do not necessarily thrive or prefer it.

## Practical relevance

This tolerance range is one reason Nile tilapia has been successfully introduced and farmed in a wide variety of water conditions worldwide, including some low-brackish or estuarine-influenced ponds where purely freshwater species would struggle. However, this project and its forecasting model are built around freshwater pond culture, which remains the dominant and best-understood commercial context for the species -- farmers with any meaningful salinity in their water source (e.g. coastal or estuarine ponds) should treat this as a caution factor worth monitoring, not an invitation to treat tilapia as a fully salt-tolerant species.

## Practical takeaway for farmers

- Occasional low-level salinity intrusion (well under 7‰) is unlikely to cause mass mortality on its own, based on this tolerance data, but is still a stressor that can compound with other water-quality problems.
- Salinity above roughly 7-9‰ is a meaningful risk threshold worth actively avoiding or correcting, based on the sharp mortality increase observed at that range in tolerance trials.
- If pond water source or intrusion patterns are known to vary in salinity (e.g. near coastal areas, during dry season concentration, or from brackish groundwater), periodic salinity testing is a reasonable precaution even though tilapia is comparatively tolerant.
